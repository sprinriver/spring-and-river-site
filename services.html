What we build, and what it changes

Five competencies, each delivered as a running system. Every use case below is one we can scope against your own data in a feasibility study before you commit to a build.

01

Data Unification

Plants rarely have a data problem in the sense of "not enough". They have a convergence problem: the historian, the LIMS, the quality register and the ERP each hold part of the answer, on different clocks, with different keys.

We build the timeseries spine that joins them — so a quality result can be traced back to the exact process conditions that produced it.

ERPCRMSCMMESHistorianData LakeDWH

UC-01 Enterprise data convergence

Timeseries-based unification across ERP, CRM, SCM and manufacturing sources. Process tags, batch genealogy and order records are aligned to a common time and asset model, so a single query can span the shop floor and the back office.

One traceable record per batch, heat or lot

UC-02 Data sourcing & processing

Raw signals are landed in a data lake at full fidelity; conformed, deduplicated and quality-checked data is published to the warehouse for BI and AI consumption. Nothing is thrown away, and nothing unvalidated reaches a dashboard.

Raw fidelity preserved, trusted layer published

UC-03 Contextualising sensor data

A tag named TI_4471 means nothing to a model. We attach asset hierarchy, units, engineering limits and product context to every signal so that features carry physical meaning and models stay interpretable to the process engineer.

Models a process engineer can argue with

UC-04 Master data reconciliation

Product codes, customer records and material grades drift apart across systems over years of operation. We reconcile them into conformed dimensions, which is usually the unglamorous step that makes every later number agree.

Finance and operations quote the same figure
02

Data Engineering

The foundation. Pipelines that run unattended, fail loudly, and can be traced from a number on a dashboard back to the instrument that produced it.

We work to medallion architectures — bronze, silver, gold — with lineage and data quality enforced end to end rather than bolted on afterwards.

MedallionStreamingLIMSELNMESD365Lineage

UC-05 Medallion architecture build-out

Bronze holds raw arrivals, silver holds cleaned and conformed entities, gold holds the business-ready marts. Each promotion is tested, versioned and reversible, so a bad upstream day does not quietly corrupt a year of reporting.

Reproducible, auditable transformations

UC-06 Real-time streaming ingestion

Streaming from PLC, SCADA and HMI layers through a streaming server into a staging database, so that models score against conditions measured seconds ago rather than a nightly extract.

Insight inside the process window

UC-07 System connectors

Purpose-built connectors to LIMS, ELN, MES, plant Historian and Dynamics 365. Instrument and laboratory systems are treated as first-class sources, not manual CSV exports maintained by one person.

Manual export steps eliminated

UC-08 Data quality & lineage enforcement

Automated expectations on ranges, freshness, completeness and referential integrity, with column-level lineage. When a KPI moves, you can tell within minutes whether the process changed or the pipeline did.

Fewer false alarms, faster root cause
03

Business Intelligence

The visibility layer. The same conformed data feeds operational, management and leadership views — so a shift supervisor and the board are looking at consistent numbers at different resolutions.

We build in Power BI where the estate is Microsoft, and in custom Plotly toolkits where the visualisation is too specialised for an off-the-shelf tile.

Power BIPlotlyKPI rollupsShop floorExecutive

UC-09 Shop-floor visualisation

Line-side displays built for glanceability under production conditions: current state, drift against spec, and the one or two variables most responsible. Designed for a decision in seconds, not an analysis session.

Operators act without leaving the line

UC-10 Executive KPI rollups

Yield, OEE, energy intensity, first-pass quality and cost per tonne rolled up across lines, plants and regions — drillable from a group number down to the individual batch that moved it.

One version of the truth, all the way down

UC-11 Custom analytical toolkits

Where standard chart types fail — microstructure overlays, multi-variate process envelopes, batch trajectory comparison — we build bespoke interactive tools that domain specialists actually want to open.

Analysis that matches the physics

UC-12 Self-service semantic layer

A governed model with agreed definitions and business logic, so that analysts across departments can answer their own questions without each rebuilding "yield" a slightly different way.

Analyst throughput without metric sprawl
04

AI & Machine Learning

The intelligence. Predictive models, timeseries analysis, computer vision and classical statistics — chosen for the problem rather than for novelty.

We have shipped gradient-boosted models onto production lines and Nextflow pipelines for genomics. The technique matters less than whether the result survives contact with the plant.

XGBoostTimeseriesComputer visionDeep learningNLPNextflow

UC-13 Process parameter optimisation

Models relate masses of historical sensor data to the output quality recorded in factory registers and quality audits. The compliant operating ranges for each parameter are established, then process control is held inside them.

Consistent output quality and higher productivity

UC-14 Predictive maintenance

Instead of correcting failures after they occur or over-servicing on a fixed calendar, intelligent algorithms predict equipment failure from inspection records, usage, environmental data, controllers and IoT devices.

Less downtime and less unnecessary maintenance

UC-15 Computer vision for quality

Deep learning on microscopy and inspection imagery: identifying constituents, textures and defects, annotating a training sample, then scoring every part against the tolerance limits that define acceptable product.

Defects caught before value is added

UC-16 Demand & energy forecasting

Timeseries models forecast power and utility demand against production targets, then recommend which combination of assets — boilers, turbines, furnaces of differing capacities — should be run to meet it at lowest cost.

Measurable fuel and energy savings

UC-17 NLP & document intelligence

Extraction of qualitative information, opinions, numerical data and tables from unstructured text — maintenance logs, deviation reports, SOPs and correspondence — turning written records into analysable fields.

Unstructured archives become queryable
05

Agentic AI

The interaction layer. MCP servers and multi-agent orchestration, built to production standards with three-layer defence-in-depth allowlisting and a full audit trail on every action.

This is shipped work, not a roadmap item — which means it comes with the boring parts: permissioning, logging, rollback and human approval gates.

MCP serversMulti-agentAllowlistingAudit trailOCR

UC-18 MCP servers over plant systems

Model Context Protocol servers expose historians, warehouses and document stores to AI assistants under explicit, reviewable permissions — so a question in natural language resolves against governed data rather than a copied spreadsheet.

Ask the plant a question, get a sourced answer

UC-19 Multi-agent orchestration

Specialised agents for retrieval, analysis, validation and reporting, coordinated so that each step is checked before the next. Every tool call passes a three-layer allowlist and is written to an immutable audit log.

Automation you can hand to an auditor

UC-20 AI agents for OCR & document workflows

Agents that read scanned certificates, batch records, mill test reports and supplier documentation, extract the fields that matter, validate them against the system of record and flag the exceptions for a human.

Hours of manual keying removed per week

UC-21 Guided remediation assistants

When a deviation is detected, the assistant assembles the context — comparable past events, what was done, what worked — and proposes a remediation path with its reasoning visible for the engineer to accept or reject.

Expertise available on every shift
